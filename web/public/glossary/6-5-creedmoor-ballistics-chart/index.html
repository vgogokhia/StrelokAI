6.5 Creedmoor ballistics chart: drop and wind to 1000 yards

A 6.5 Creedmoor 140 gr ELD Match at 2710 fps with a 100-yard zero needs about 2.9 MIL (10.1 MOA) at 500 yards and 8.8 MIL (30.4 MOA) at 1000 yards at sea level. A 10 mph crosswind moves it about 68 inches at 1000 yards, against 112 inches for a .308 175 gr.

6.5 Creedmoor vs .308

The 6.5 mm bullet's higher ballistic coefficient (G7 0.326 vs 0.243) means it drifts about 39% less at 1000 yards, stays supersonic past 1100 yards, and recoils less. The .308 has more barrel life and a wider range of cheap ammunition.

Barrel and twist

Factory 6.5 Creedmoor rifles use a 1:8 twist (sometimes 1:7.5), needed for long 140–147 gr bullets. Our solver gives a Miller stability factor of 1.59 for the 140 gr ELD Match in 1:8 at sea level. Box velocities come from 24-inch test barrels; from a 20–22 inch barrel expect 2600–2700 fps, and measure it.

FAQ

How far can a 6.5 Creedmoor shoot accurately?

Good match loads stay supersonic to roughly 1200–1400 yards at sea level, and competitors regularly hit targets at 1000 yards and beyond.

How much does a 6.5 Creedmoor drop at 500 yards?

About 53 inches with a 140 gr bullet at 2710 fps and a 100-yard zero, or 2.9 MIL / 10.1 MOA.

Is 6.5 Creedmoor better than .308 for long range?

For target shooting past 600 yards, yes: less wind drift, less drop and less recoil. For hunting inside 400 yards the practical difference is small.

What is the best zero for 6.5 Creedmoor?

A 100-yard zero is standard for long-range shooting because it is easy to confirm and every calculator and DOPE card assumes it. Hunters sometimes prefer a 200-yard zero.

What twist rate does 6.5 Creedmoor need?

A 1:8 twist stabilizes the popular 140–147 gr bullets. Very long bullets such as the 156 gr Berger EOL prefer 1:7.5 or 1:7.
