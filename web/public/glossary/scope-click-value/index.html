Scope click value: how many clicks is 1 MOA or 1 MIL?

A ¼ MOA scope needs 4 clicks per MOA, and each click moves the impact about 0.26 inches at 100 yards. A 0.1 MIL scope needs 10 clicks per MIL, and each click moves the impact 0.36 inches at 100 yards, or 1 cm at 100 meters.

Updated September 30, 2026 · ballistics.ge

Click values at common distances

Most hunting scopes use ¼ MOA. Most tactical and precision scopes use 0.1 MIL. The value is engraved on the turret cap ("1 click = ¼ MOA" or "0.1 MRAD").

Converting a come-up to clicks

clicks = come-up ÷ click value

A 3.5 MIL come-up on a 0.1 MIL turret is 35 clicks. A 12 MOA come-up on a ¼ MOA turret is 48 clicks. The calculator shows clicks directly once you choose your click value in Settings.

For big come-ups, know how much one turret revolution is worth, often 10 MIL or 15–25 MOA. A .308 at 1000 yards needs about 12.0 MIL, more than one full turn on a 10 MIL turret, so the turret's revolution indicator or zero stop is important.

Why you should verify your click value

Turrets are not always perfect. A "box test" or "tall target test" checks them. Put a tall sheet with a vertical line at 100 yards, fire a group, dial up exactly 10 MIL (or 30 MOA), fire again, and measure the distance between group centers. 10 MIL should be exactly 36.0 inches at 100 yards (or 100 cm at 100 m). If it is 35.2 inches, your turret moves 2% less than labeled, and every long-range come-up will be short.

Also remember that "cm clicks" are sometimes printed on European scopes: a "1 cm at 100 m" click is exactly 0.1 MIL.

FAQ

How many clicks is 1 inch at 100 yards?

About 4 clicks on a ¼ MOA scope (each click is 0.26 inches), or about 2.8 clicks on a 0.1 MIL scope (each click is 0.36 inches).

How many clicks to move 1 inch at 50 yards?

At 50 yards each click moves half as much, so about 8 clicks on a ¼ MOA scope or about 5.5 clicks on a 0.1 MIL scope.

How many MOA is one turn of the turret?

It depends on the scope; common values are 15, 20 or 25 MOA per turn for MOA scopes and 10 MIL per turn for MIL scopes. It is in the manual and often on the turret.

Which way do I turn the turret?

Turn toward the arrow marked UP to move the impact up, and toward R to move it right. You adjust in the direction you want the bullet to go.
