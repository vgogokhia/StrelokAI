Transonic range: what happens when a bullet goes subsonic

The transonic zone is the speed range from roughly Mach 1.2 down to Mach 0.8, around 1340 to 900 fps at sea level. There the airflow around the bullet is partly supersonic and partly subsonic, drag changes sharply, and some bullets wobble. Predictions become less reliable, so long-range loads are chosen to stay supersonic.

Why it matters

  • Drag spikes. Drag coefficient rises steeply as a bullet approaches Mach 1, so a small error in BC or velocity becomes a big error in drop.
  • Stability. The shock wave moving along the bullet can upset marginally stable bullets and open groups. A good stability factor helps.
  • Model limits. BC values are least accurate here, which is why truing at long range matters.

Our calculator warns when the bullet is transonic at the target, and skips the warning for loads that start subsonic.

Subsonic ammunition

Subsonic loads start below the speed of sound and never cross it. Standard-velocity and subsonic .22 LR, and .300 Blackout subsonic loads, are chosen for this reason: they are quieter, especially with a suppressor, and avoid the accuracy penalty of passing through Mach 1. High-velocity .22 LR at about 1250 fps goes transonic within the first 50–100 yards, which is one reason match shooters use standard velocity.

FAQ

At what speed does a bullet go subsonic?

Below the local speed of sound: about 1116 fps (340 m/s) at 59 °F, about 1090 fps at 32 °F, and about 1150 fps at 90 °F.

Is it bad if my bullet goes subsonic before the target?

It is not automatically a miss, but accuracy and prediction quality often drop. For long-range work, pick a load that stays above about Mach 1.1–1.2 at your longest target.

Why do subsonic .22 LR rounds shoot more accurately?

They never pass through the speed of sound, so they avoid the buffeting and drag changes of the transonic zone.

Can a calculator predict the transonic zone correctly?

Roughly. The G7 drag model tracks long boat-tail bullets through Mach 1 better than G1, and truing the BC close to the transonic range improves predictions. Expect somewhat more vertical dispersion there than at supersonic speeds.
