Mil-dot range estimation: how to range with your reticle

To range with a reticle, measure the target in MIL and use range (m) = target size (cm) × 10 ÷ MIL, or range (yd) = target size (in) × 27.78 ÷ MIL. With MOA use range (yd) = size (in) × 95.5 ÷ MOA. It only works when you know the target's real size.

Updated September 30, 2026 · ballistics.ge

The mil-relation formula

A milliradian is 1/1000 of the distance. So an object 1 meter tall that measures 1 MIL is 1000 meters away. Rearranged for everyday units:

range (m) = size (cm) × 10 ÷ MIL
range (yd) = size (in) × 27.78 ÷ MIL
range (yd) = size (in) × 95.5 ÷ MOA

How to get a good reading

  • In an SFP scope, only range at the calibrated magnification. At half power every mark is worth double. See FFP vs SFP.
  • Brace the rifle and measure to tenths of a MIL. At 500 m on a 45 cm target, an error of 0.1 MIL in the reading is 50 m of range error.
  • Measure the dimension you know best. Heights of standing animals vary; the back-to-brisket depth of an adult deer is more consistent.
  • Measure twice, from different edges, and average.

Reticle ranging is a backup skill. Past 400–500 yards, a laser rangefinder is far more accurate, and range errors turn directly into vertical misses: with a .308 at 600 yards, 25 yards of range error is about 0.6 MIL, or 13 inches.

FAQ

How accurate is mil-dot ranging?

Under field conditions expect roughly ±5–10% error. That is fine for flat-shooting cartridges inside 400 yards, but not enough for first-round hits at long range.

What does the 27.78 in the formula mean?

It converts inches and yards into the mil relation: 1000 ÷ 36 = 27.78. In meters and centimeters the constant is simply 10.

Can I range with a MOA reticle?

Yes. Use range (yd) = size (in) × 95.5 ÷ MOA, or range (m) = size (cm) × 34.4 ÷ MOA.

What if my reticle has no dots, only hash marks?

Hash marks work the same way. Most modern MIL reticles have marks every 0.2 or 0.5 MIL with finer subdivisions near the center, which makes readings more precise than classic dots.
