Density altitude: how air density changes your trajectory

Density altitude is the altitude in the standard atmosphere that has the same air density as your current conditions. Thinner air (high, hot, low pressure) means less drag, so the bullet drops less. A .308 175 gr needs 12.0 MIL at 1000 yards at sea level but only 10.1 MIL at 5000 ft and 90 °F.

Updated September 30, 2026 · ballistics.ge

What changes air density

  • Altitude and pressure: the biggest factor. Air at 5000 ft is about 15% thinner than at sea level.
  • Temperature: warm air is thinner. A 40 °F rise lowers density by about 8%. Temperature also changes muzzle velocity through powder temperature.
  • Humidity: humid air is slightly thinner (water vapor is lighter than dry air), but the effect is small.

Station pressure vs barometric pressure

The pressure on weather reports is "sea-level corrected" barometric pressure. For ballistics you need station pressure, the actual pressure where you stand. At 3000 ft they differ by about 3 inHg (100 mbar). If you enter the weather-report value, also enter your altitude; if you enter station pressure from a Kestrel or similar, the altitude is already included. FAQ

Does a bullet drop less at high altitude?

Yes. Thinner air slows the bullet less, so it arrives faster and drops less. The difference is small at 300 yards but large at 1000 yards.

Does temperature affect bullet drop?

Yes, in two ways: warm air is thinner, and warm powder usually gives higher muzzle velocity. Both reduce drop.

Should I use density altitude or pressure and temperature?

Either works if entered correctly. Density altitude is a convenient single number; entering station pressure and temperature separately also lets the calculator adjust muzzle velocity for temperature.

Does humidity affect trajectory?

Slightly. Humid air is a little less dense, so drop is slightly smaller, but the effect is far smaller than temperature or altitude.

What is a normal density altitude?

At sea level on a standard 59 °F day it is 0 ft. On a hot summer day at low elevation it is often 2000–3000 ft, and in the mountains it can exceed 8000 ft.
